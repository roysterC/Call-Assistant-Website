## 1. The five feed ads, scored

Tier is the skill's reach ranking for static formats: S/A reach cold audiences, B converts people who already know the problem or the brand.

| Ad | Template | Tier | Reader stage | Verdict | Change |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Your new front desk | Lifestyle hero + callout (the call card) | B | Problem-aware | Strong. The card shows the result (booked, text sent) in one glance. | Caption: split the long last sentence (below). |
| 2. Both hands busy | Headline statement + lifestyle photo | B | Problem-aware | Best cold opener of the five: names a moment every stylist has had. | Lead with this one. |
| 3. Fewer empty chairs | Feature spotlight (real texts) | B | Product-aware | Better for people who already know Kikai: retargeting and profile visitors. | Caption: drop the paired fragments (below). |
| 4. Answers WhatsApp too | Feature spotlight (real screen) | B | Solution-aware | Clear. The phone text is small at feed size. | Fine for feed; for Stories, crop to the conversation. |
| 5. Switching is the easy part | Competitor callout | B | Product-aware | Speaks to the biggest fear (losing client data). | Keep it organic or for retargeting. In paid ads, naming Fresha and others can draw review scrutiny; the variant without names is safer. |

**The gap:** all five are B tier (now covered by the founder ad, concept C). The skill's most reliable format for reaching new people is **founder content**: the person behind Kikai, on camera or in a short note, saying why they built it. It needs your story, so it's in the questions at the end.

### Caption fixes (AI tells)
- **Ad 1** had a sentence that kept going after its point ("…and books it, and a text confirmation goes out straight away"). Now: "Kikai answers in your salon’s name, finds a free slot and books it. The client gets a text confirmation straight away."
- **Ad 3** ended on two matching fragments ("Fewer forgotten appointments, fewer gaps in the diary."). Now: "That means fewer forgotten appointments and fewer gaps in your diary."
- Ads 2, 4 and 5 pass.

## 3. New concepts to make next

### Concept A: FAQ card (tier B, objection handling)
- **Headline on image:** “Am I talking to a real person?”
- **Body on image:** It says no, plainly. Then it books the appointment.
- **Visual:** Olive background, the question in a cream speech bubble, the answer below in Geist, logo bottom left. Same layout as ad 5.
- **Grounding:** `site.ts` calls list ("Says no, plainly, then keeps helping") and the FAQ "Will callers know it’s not a person?".
- **Why:** this is the objection most owners will have; FAQ cards work fast on people already weighing it up.

### Concept B: WhatsApp conversation (tier B, conversation treatment)
- **Visual:** the real WhatsApp thread from the app, full height, cropped to the four messages: “Can I get in with Jess on Thursday for a cut and blow dry?” → “Jess has 10:00 or 14:30…” → “10 please” → “You’re booked in…”
- **Headline:** Booked over WhatsApp at 8:30pm.
- **Grounding:** the demo screenshot `inbox-phone.jpg`. It's demo data, so the caption should not present it as a real client.

## 4. What to test (hypotheses that can lose)

While you post organically, compare like for like: same week, same time of day, similar captions.

1. **Ad 2 beats ad 1 on profile visits per 1,000 views** among salon-owner followers. If ad 1 wins, the after-hours angle is the stronger opener and leads the next batch.
2. **The FAQ card (concept A) beats ad 3 on saves and DMs.** If it loses, answering objections belongs in sales calls, not the feed.
3. **Once paid ads run:** angle 1 (missed calls) beats angle 4 (switching) on click-through for cold UK salon owners; angle 4 beats angle 1 on cost per booked call in retargeting.

Log each result in the Learnings table of `.agents/product-marketing.md` so the next batch starts from it.
